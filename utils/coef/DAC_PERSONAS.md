## Persona 1 — dCS Vivaldi Apex

**Sonic Character: "Analytical Precision with Organic Resolution"**

The dCS house sound is about revealing everything in the recording without ever sounding harsh.
It is surgical but not clinical. The Vivaldi Apex is known for an enormous sense of space and
air — you hear deep into the mix. The baseline apodising filter (Kaiser β=8, fc=20.5 kHz) sits
just above 20 kHz to remove the Gibbs ringing injected by CD mastering brickwall filters
upstream, which is dCS's core philosophy. The relaxed beta means the transition band is gentle —
not trying to be a brickwall itself — giving a relaxed, airy quality rather than the "tense"
sound of a very steep filter.

| Variation | Parameters | Description |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| F1 | Kaiser β=8, fc=20.5k, linear | Definitive dCS filter — apodising, removes upstream pre-ringing |
| F2 | Kaiser β=12, fc=20k, linear | More frequency-domain precision, slightly more "hi-fi" |
| F3 | Kaiser β=5, fc=20.5k, linear | Softer — more analogue-like ease, less defined but more natural |
| F4 | Blackman-Harris, fc=19k, linear | Deep apodising — maximum removal of digital artefacts |
| F5 | Kaiser β=8, fc=20.5k, hybrid | Organic quality of minimum-phase combined with dCS openness |
| F6 | Kaiser β=8, fc=23k, linear | "Air and space" filter — maximises perception of presence |

## Persona 2 — Chord DAVE

**Sonic Character: "Transient Accuracy and Maximum Tap Count"**

Rob Watts's WTA (Watts Transient Alignment) algorithm is the defining characteristic. The DAVE
uses over 1 million taps to reconstruct the waveform with extraordinary precision. The result is
that you hear micro-details — the precise decay of a piano note, the exact attack of a plucked
string — that other DACs smear together. It is not warm, it is not smooth — it is truthful. The
steep Kaiser β=14 brickwall is intentional: keep 20 kHz as a hard boundary, waste no energy on
the transition, put all the taps into passband accuracy.

| Variation | Parameters | Description |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| F1 | Kaiser β=14, fc=20k, linear | WTA reference — tight brickwall, maximum impulse accuracy |
| F2 | Kaiser β=18, fc=20k, linear | Even more stopband rejection, most "hi-fi" presentation |
| F3 | Kaiser β=10, fc=20k, linear | Relaxed — trades some precision for a less intense listen |
| F4 | Blackman-Harris, fc=18.5k, linear | Cleans up CD-era material, less fatiguing on older recordings |
| F5 | Kaiser β=14, fc=20k, minimum | Removes pre-ringing entirely — fundamentally different character |
| F6 | Kaiser β=14, fc=22k, linear | Adds "air" that the strict brickwall cuts off |

## Persona 3 — MSB Reference

**Sonic Character: "Zero Pre-Ringing, Organic Transients, Black Background"**

MSB uses a discrete R-2R ladder DAC — no delta-sigma, no DSD conversion. The modulator noise
floor is exceptionally low, giving an almost spooky quiet background. The sonic character is
organic and natural — transients have the right energy envelope (all post-impulse, nothing before
it), which is exactly what you hear in real acoustic instruments. Minimum phase is the correct
choice here: no pre-ringing means a piano attack sounds like a piano attack, not like something
that starts ringing before the hammer hits the string. The Hann window gives a smooth,
non-aggressive roll-off.

| Variation | Parameters | Description |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| F1 | Hann, fc=20k, minimum | MSB reference — Hann + minimum phase, zero pre-ringing |
| F2 | Kaiser β=10, fc=20k, minimum | Slightly steeper, more defined, still zero pre-ringing |
| F3 | Kaiser β=6, fc=20k, minimum | Softest version, most "analogue" feeling |
| F4 | Blackman-Harris, fc=19k, minimum | Deep apodising and zero pre-ringing combined |
| F5 | Kaiser β=10, fc=20k, hybrid | Compromise — some pre-ringing returns, sharper transient attack |
| F6 | Hann, fc=23k, minimum | More air without introducing pre-ringing |

## Persona 4 — Berkeley Audio Design Alpha 3

**Sonic Character: "Maximum Analogue Character, No Digital Fingerprint"**

Michael Ritter's philosophy at Berkeley is radical: the DAC chip does nothing but convert bits
to voltage. All the filtering happens in the FPGA. This means the output has no trace of the
internal chip filter (which typically adds its own phase distortion and roll-off character). The
Berkeley sound is extraordinarily open and relaxed — non-fatiguing, listeners report being able
to sit with it for hours. The aggressive FIRLS + Blackman-Harris apodising at 18.5 kHz means the
filter is already rolled off before 20 kHz. This sounds like a limitation but the ear perceives
it as ease — there is no "edge" to the sound.

| Variation | Parameters | Description |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| F1 | FIRLS+BH, fc=18.5k, linear | Berkeley reference — maximum analogue ease |
| F2 | FIRLS+BH, fc=20k, linear | Widens to 20 kHz — more detail at the cost of some ease |

| F5 | FIRLS+BH, fc=18.5k, hybrid | Adds minimum-phase organic quality to the Berkeley character |

# Filter Design Choices: The Science and the Honesty

## What We Actually Know vs. What We Are Approximating

### The Hard Truth First

We do **not** know with confidence exactly what Rob Watts, dCS, MSB, or Mola Mola are doing
internally. What we have is:

- **Published patents** (Watts has several on WTA, dCS has SACD/ring DAC patents)
- **White papers** (dCS Ring DAC, Chord WTA algorithm description)
- **Measurements** published by Audio Science Review, Stereophile, and others — impulse
  responses, frequency responses, phase responses
- **Interviews** where designers describe their philosophy in general terms
- **Reverse-engineering** from those measurements by the audio community

What we are doing is **emulating the audible fingerprint** of those designs using filter theory,
not replicating their actual silicon. The distinction matters.

---

## Why Specific Windows Produce Specific Sounds

The relationship between window choice and audible character is well-understood DSP. The
science here is solid.

### Kaiser Window — Why Chord DAVE uses high beta

The Kaiser window is parametric — beta controls the **trade-off between stopband attenuation
and transition band width**. High beta (14–20) means:

```
Passband → [very flat] → steep cliff → [very deep stopband]
```

Rob Watts has explicitly stated in interviews and white papers that his WTA filter prioritises
**time domain accuracy over frequency domain performance**. The paradox is that to get time
domain accuracy you need **very long filters** (~1 million taps), not necessarily steep
frequency roll-off. However, the Kaiser window's shape produces a very controlled impulse
response — the ringing is symmetric and decays predictably. With enough taps, the pre-ringing
and post-ringing are pushed so far out in time that they become inaudible. This is the WTA
principle: **don't reduce the ringing, make it temporally inaudible by spreading it far from
the transient**.

A Hamming window at the same tap count would have lower stopband attenuation (~43 dB vs ~80+
dB for Kaiser β=14) and a less controlled transition. Kaiser gives **adjustable precision** —
that is why it dominates serious filter design.

### Hann Window — Why MSB uses it for minimum phase

The Hann window has a **smooth, gradual roll-off shape**:

```
Passband → [gentle slope] → quiet stopband
```

Stopband attenuation is ~44 dB — less than Kaiser β=14 — but the **impulse response is very
smooth** with no abrupt discontinuities. Combined with minimum-phase conversion (which takes
all the energy and pushes it forward in time, eliminating pre-ringing entirely), the Hann
window produces the most **organically-shaped post-impulse decay**.

The minimum-phase conversion via Hilbert transform or homomorphic method does not change the
magnitude response — only the phase. So you keep the Hann's gentle frequency roll-off but
eliminate all pre-ringing. The remaining ringing all follows the impulse, which is how acoustic
instruments actually behave in a room. MSB have published that their design philosophy is
explicitly around this — zero pre-ringing, all energy causal.

### Blackman-Harris Window — Why it is used for apodising

Blackman-Harris has the **highest stopband attenuation** of the common windows (~92 dB) with a
fairly wide transition band. The wide transition is the key:

```
Passband → [wide gentle slope starting at ~17–19 kHz] → very deep stopband
```

The word **apodising** literally means "removing the foot" — it refers to removing the
sidelobes of the sinc function that cause Gibbs phenomenon ringing. A CD's original mastering
uses a brickwall at 22.05 kHz which injects its own pre-ringing into the signal. An apodising
filter rolls off *before* that brickwall frequency, effectively erasing that pre-ringing.

dCS's apodising filters (and our F4 variations) do exactly this. dCS has explicitly described
this in their technical literature — their filter is designed to attenuate the out-of-band
energy that causes Gibbs ringing from upstream 44.1 kHz brickwall filters.

### FIRLS + Blackman-Harris — Why Berkeley uses it

FIRLS (Least Squares FIR) is different from window-based design — instead of windowing a sinc
function, it **minimises the weighted least-squares error** across defined frequency bands. This
gives explicit control over the passband ripple and stopband attenuation trade-off. Berkeley's
approach (as described by Michael Ritter) is to design the filter optimally for the specific
stopband weight, then apply Blackman-Harris to smooth the window and ensure the apodising
character. The combination gives a filter that is both **mathematically optimal in the
stopband** and **perceptually gentle in the transition band**.

### Gaussian Window — Why Mola Mola's F6 uses it

A Gaussian window has **no sidelobes** — it is the minimum time-bandwidth product window. This
means the impulse response and the frequency response are both as compact as the uncertainty
principle allows. Bruno Putzeys's background is in control theory and DSP optimisation — a
Gaussian roll-off for the extended bandwidth variant gives the most natural-sounding "air"
because there is no ringing associated with the roll-off at all. The price is that the stopband
attenuation is modest, but for an "extended air" variant above 20 kHz that is acceptable.

## What We Know About Each Designer's Actual Approach

### Rob Watts / Chord

**Confidence: High on philosophy, moderate on implementation detail.**

Watts has published extensively. Known facts:
- WTA algorithm published in AES papers
- Filter length: confirmed >1 million taps in DAVE (FPGA)
- Linear phase: explicitly confirmed — he considers minimum phase "wrong"
- The filter prioritises **time domain reconstruction accuracy** — specifically the ability to
  reconstruct the exact position of a transient in time
- Kaiser-type windowing consistent with his stated stopband requirements

What we do not know: exact beta values, exact tap count per stage, whether there is
frequency-dependent weighting.

### dCS

**Confidence: High on apodising philosophy, moderate on exact parameters.**

dCS publishes white papers on their Ring DAC and filter philosophy. Known facts:
- They explicitly offer apodising vs non-apodising filter modes
- Their F1 is described as "slow roll-off linear phase" — consistent with low Kaiser beta
- They explicitly state the apodising filter removes 44.1 kHz brickwall pre-ringing
- Six filters shipped in Vivaldi: confirmed in product documentation

What we do not know: exact window functions, exact cutoff frequencies (we estimate from
published frequency response measurements).

### MSB

**Confidence: High on minimum phase philosophy, low on implementation.**

MSB is the least open about their filter design. What is known:
- They explicitly market "minimum phase filtering" as a feature
- Impulse response measurements published by reviewers with AP equipment show zero pre-ringing
  — confirming minimum phase
- The smooth roll-off in measurements is consistent with Hann or a similar smooth window

What we do not know: almost everything else.

### Berkeley Audio Design

**Confidence: High on philosophy, moderate on implementation.**

Michael Ritter has given interviews explicitly describing:
- The FPGA does all filtering; the DAC chip is bypassed for filtering
- They use apodising to remove upstream brickwall artefacts
- The philosophy of "the DAC chip should just convert"

FIRLS is our inference from the measured frequency response shape — it has the characteristic
least-squares ripple pattern that distinguishes it from windowed sinc designs.

### Mola Mola / Bruno Putzeys

**Confidence: Moderate on philosophy, low on filter specifics.**

Putzeys has published on the MDAT modulator (the noise shaper) but less on the reconstruction
filter. His engineering background suggests:
- Maximum dynamic range as the primary goal
- Steep linear phase filter consistent with his background in professional audio
- Kaiser high-beta is a reasonable inference from published measurements

### Merason

**Confidence: Low — least documentation available.**

Merason is a small Swiss company with minimal published technical detail. The PCM1794A chip has
its own internal 8× oversampling filter, and our external filter is designed to complement it
rather than conflict with it. Kaiser β=11 is an engineering choice based on what the Burr-Brown
/ TI datasheet suggests about the internal filter's characteristics.

## Summary: Confidence Table

| Claim | Confidence |
|-------|-----------|
| Window function → frequency/phase behaviour relationship | **Very high** — textbook DSP |
| Minimum phase → zero pre-ringing | **Certain** — mathematically proven |
| Apodising → removes CD Gibbs artefacts | **Certain** — well understood |
| dCS uses apodising philosophy | **High** — they publish it |
| Chord uses linear phase, very long filters | **High** — Watts has published it |
| MSB uses minimum phase | **High** — measurements confirm it |
| Our specific beta/cutoff values match theirs exactly | **Low** — approximated from measurements and philosophy |
| Our 64-tap URAM filter sounds like a million-tap DAVE | **No** — tap count dominates WTA quality |

The honest engineering position: we are creating **filter personas that capture the same
audible philosophy** as each reference DAC — same phase behaviour, same roll-off character,
same apodising or non-apodising intent. The exact numerical parameters are engineering
approximations, not reverse-engineered clones. At the tap counts running in simulation (4096
total taps), the perceptual differences between personas are real and audible; claiming they
sound identical to the hardware originals would be false.
